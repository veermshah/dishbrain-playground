What is this?

DishBrain

In 2022 Brett Kagan and colleagues at Cortical Labs grew about 800,000 human and mouse neurons on a high-density multi-electrode array and wired them into a game of Pong. Electrical stimulation told the culture where the ball was; activity in "motor" regions moved the paddle. Over a 20-minute session, cultures that received feedback rallied longer in the later minutes than in the first five, and longer than cultures without feedback.

The free-energy idea

The feedback was not a reward. After a miss, the culture got unpredictable stimulation (random sites, 5 Hz, 4 s). After a hit it got a short predictable burst. The hypothesis, from Karl Friston's free-energy principle, is that a self-organising network acts to make its inputs predictable, so it should drift towards behaviour that avoids the chaotic noise.

This simulation

1000 Izhikevich neurons (80% excitatory, 20% inhibitory), sparse random wiring, delays up to 10 ms and spike-timing-dependent plasticity on excitatory synapses. Each neuron sits under one of 64 virtual electrodes. Two central columns are sensory: the row encodes where the ball is relative to the paddle (place code) and the pulse rate encodes how close it is (4–40 Hz). Columns 0–1 push the paddle up, columns 6–7 push it down.

Controls

Predictable
After a miss, all 16 sensory electrodes pulse together at 100 Hz for 100 ms.
Unpredictable
After a miss, random subsets of sensory electrodes pulse at random times (5 Hz, 2× amplitude) for the whole 2 s pause.
Silent
After a miss, the game pauses for 2 s with no stimulation at all.
STDP
Turns plasticity on or off. With it off, nothing can be learned.
Speed
1× is real time. The model usually runs faster than 50× real time.
Run experiment
Runs every condition for N seeds in background workers, then plots mean rally length with 95% confidence bands and Mann-Whitney U tests.

Honest caveat

This is a toy model, not a culture. In my runs, plain STDP does not produce the learning effect seen in the paper; see the README for the numbers and why I think that is.
